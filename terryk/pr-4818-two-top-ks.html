Five pages on this PR, one question each — this one: which "top-k" is which.

Background. At each generated token vLLM has a probability for every token in the vocabulary. A sampling filter (top_k, top_p) cuts that list before sampling; the tokens that survive are the support. Separately, vLLM can report the probabilities of its K most likely tokens (top_logprobs=K) without cutting anything. #4818's "top-k" is the second kind. Dotted words have a short explanation: hover or tap them.

The "top-p is not supported yet" you heard comes from mask replay, not from #4818. vLLM's replay requires top_k > 0: "pure top-p can produce vocab-sized masks". So top-p works only together with top-k, never alone. #4818 forbids both filters for a different reason. Its loss models vLLM's probabilities outside the K recorded tokens as a rescaled copy of the trainer's full softmax. A filter would set those probabilities to zero, which breaks that model.

So what. #4818 does not move a sampling mask, and it does not need Gym's help with one. Its K candidates would only meet mask replay if the two were combined later, and the loss would then need rework to model the tail inside the kept support. The real gap is row 2. Today, a sampled token that the trainer's own top-p cut excludes is dropped from the loss; the only trace is the policy_support_excluded_tokens count. The fix is mask replay (#3937), and the next page shows how its mask would have to travel once token capture is on.
