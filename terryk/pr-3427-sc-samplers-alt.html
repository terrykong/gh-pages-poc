Reading it back

in_order — “you get what was promised to you”
The stamp is assigned at dispatch, before the rollout runs, so a group's destiny never changes no matter when it finishes. g4 finishing early buys it nothing; g2/g3 being weight-stale costs them nothing. This is the legacy async-GRPO pattern: legacy ReplayBuffer.sample() filters to the age window, then narrows to target_weight_versions[i] == current_weight_version, stalling unless a full batch is intended for this step — "so no trajectory loses its last chance to be used for its intended training step." legacy replay_buffer.py L250-271 · InOrderSampler.select L359
Same answer ≠ same rule
Three of the four modes pick g2 + g3 here. That is not a flaw in the example — it is the honest result, and it is worth showing: under a gated pump the modes usually agree, and they diverge only when something finishes out of order. Flip to windowed + freshest-first to see the one case where the picture changes.
sample_freshest_first changes the set, not the order
It sorts by lag before the max_prompt_groups cap bites, so g4 — a group generated for step 2 — gets pulled forward, and g3 is left to age out. Same buffer, same window, different training data. L249
“Isn’t there a FIFO where everything is used and nothing is evicted?”
Yes — in_order with min_groups_for_streaming_train = num_prompts_per_step, which is what the sync recipe sets. _finalize_selection returns None below min_prompt_groups, so the trainer waits at step N until the whole batch stamped N has landed, then takes all of it. Its evict only fires for a group that commits after its step has gone by — like g5 if it ran two steps long. So the mode already exists; the guide just never says in_order is the zero-waste option and windowed the deliberately-lossy one. The cost is the mirror image: in_order idles the trainer on the slowest group in the batch, which is exactly what windowed exists to avoid.
