Which GPUs run the policy, the critic, and generation

Companion illustration for NVIDIA-NeMo/RL #3773 — PPO on the SingleController path. All permalinks pin the PR head 90c48c9.

PPO runs three GPU jobs at once: generation writes completions, the policy learns from them, and the critic — a second trained model, new on this path — predicts how much reward each completion will earn. On the SingleController path the split is fixed, not tuned: generation always gets its own GPUs, and the policy and critic always share. That sharing is why a PPO step is a sequence of copies to and from CPU memory.

Where each one runs

The SingleController guide requires policy.generation.colocated.enabled: false as a mandatory setting — rollout is driven through generate_and_push, which only works on the disaggregated async engine. So generation is always on separate GPUs. _build_clusters (setup.py:132) then hands the critic to the training side with one line: train_worker_groups = 2 if is_ppo_run(...) else 1 (:146).

Both PPO configs this PR ships follow that. The small exemplar takes a single 2-GPU node and gives generation one GPU, leaving one for policy and critic together. The nightly recipe takes two nodes of 8 GPUs and gives generation a whole node, leaving the other node's 8 GPUs to policy and critic together.

A caveat worth knowing. _build_clusters still carries a colocated branch that would stack all three on one cluster (:163), and this PR extends its arithmetic so the critic is counted there too. But that is cluster sizing, not a working mode: the guide requires colocated off, the SingleController actor has no way to put generation to sleep while training runs, and no shipped config turns it on. Nothing in the code rejects colocated.enabled: true either, so it is a documented requirement with no check behind it.

One RL step on the training GPUs

Config for this walkthrough — every number distinct, so any digit traces back to one source:

settingvaluewhy it matters here
num_prompts_per_step3prompts drawn per step
num_generations_per_prompt4completions per prompt
rows in the step (3 × 4)12must equal value.train_global_batch_size, or setup refuses to start
ppo_epochs2times both models step their optimizer on those same 12 rows

The same 12 rows are used twice. Nothing is re-generated and nothing is re-sharded — the loop just steps both optimizers again on the batch in hand:

for epoch in range(2): # ppo_epochs = 2 :1052 epoch 0 — critic step 1/2 _value_train(12 rows) :1056 policy step 1/2 train + finish_train_step :1093 copy policy out offload_to_cpu() # 0 < 2-1 :1100 epoch 1 — critic step 2/2 _value_train(12 rows) :1056 policy step 2/2 train + finish_train_step :1093 (no copy-out) # 1 is not < 2-1 # per RL step: 2 critic optimizer steps, 2 policy optimizer steps, 12 rows, 1 chunk

Two rules keep that arithmetic honest, both checked before any GPU is allocated: one RL step must arrive as a single chunk (config.py:802), and the 12 rows must equal what the critic is configured to train on (:860). Without the second, the critic would quietly step once per chunk while the policy stepped once per step — two different learning rates from one config file, and no error.

Does it work?

Yes, with a real run. The author posted convergence curves on the PR: the legacy PPO path at the same settings, this PR, and a third run resumed from a step-20 checkpoint. Reward and token_mult_prob_error track the legacy path and the critic's loss falls cleanly. The run is the two-node nightly recipe added here, which is also the config that exercises everything new — ppo_epochs: 4, ten steps of critic-only warmup, and a widened generation lookahead during that warmup.

Two honest gaps in that evidence:

  • train/critic/explained_var is not in the posted panel, even though this PR ships a nightly threshold on it. That is the number separating a critic that learned something from one that collapsed to predicting the batch average — falling loss alone does not tell them apart.
  • Throughput is not comparable to the legacy path: total_flops is recorded from the last epoch only, so above ppo_epochs: 1 it under-reports by that factor. The PR says so plainly.
What to take away. Generation's placement is yours to choose; the policy and critic sharing GPUs is not. That sharing is what the copies pay for, and they are not free — ppo_epochs: 4 means four rounds of moving the policy out and back per step, so raising it buys extra optimizer steps at the price of extra host-device traffic. Generation is unaffected — it has its own GPUs and keeps running through the hand-off. If you are porting a PPO config, the two rules above — one chunk per step, and critic batch size equal to prompts × generations — are what setup will reject you for, and colocated.enabled must stay false, which setup will not tell you.
