How one RL step trains two models on the same GPUs

Companion illustration for NVIDIA-NeMo/RL #3773 — PPO on the SingleController path. All permalinks pin the PR head 90c48c9.

GRPO scores a completion by comparing it to others in its group, so it trains one model. PPO instead learns a second model — the critic, which predicts how much reward a completion will earn — and trains it alongside the policy. This PR puts both on the SingleController path, and both land on the same training GPUs. They never fit at once, so the step is a hand-off: whoever is not working gets copied out to CPU memory. The whole design is that sequence of moves.

What ppo_epochs actually loops over

The same 12 rows are used twice. Nothing is re-generated and nothing is re-sharded — the loop just steps both optimizers again on the batch already in hand. Traced with the numbers above:

for epoch in range(2): # ppo_epochs = 2 :1052 epoch 0 ─ critic step 1/2 _value_train(12 rows) :1056 policy step 1/2 train + finish_train_step :1093 copy policy out offload_to_cpu() :1100 # runs because epoch 0 < 2-1 epoch 1 ─ critic step 2/2 _value_train(12 rows) :1056 policy step 2/2 train + finish_train_step :1093 (no copy-out) # epoch 1 is not < 2-1 # per RL step: 2 critic optimizer steps, 2 policy optimizer steps, 12 rows, 1 chunk

Two rules keep that arithmetic honest, both checked before any GPU is allocated. One RL step must arrive as a single chunk (config.py:802), and the 12 rows must exactly equal what the critic is configured to train on (:860). Without the second rule the critic would quietly step its optimizer once per chunk while the policy stepped once per step — two different learning rates from one config file, and no error.

Does it work?

Yes, with a real run. The author posted convergence curves on the PR: the legacy PPO path at the same settings, this PR, and a third run resumed from a step-20 checkpoint. Reward and token_mult_prob_error track the legacy path, and the critic's loss falls cleanly. The run is the 2-node, 8-GPU-per-node nightly recipe added by this PR, which is also the config that exercises everything new here — ppo_epochs: 4, 10 steps of critic-only warmup, and the widened generation lookahead.

Two honest gaps in that evidence:

  • train/critic/explained_var is not in the posted panel, even though this PR ships a nightly threshold on it. That is the number that separates a critic which has learned something from one that has collapsed to predicting the batch average — falling loss alone does not tell them apart.
  • Throughput here is not comparable to the legacy path: total_flops is recorded from the last epoch only, so with ppo_epochs above 1 it under-reports by that factor. The PR says so plainly.
What to take away. PPO now runs on the SingleController path with convergence matching the legacy path, and the cost of the second model is paid in copies rather than in GPUs — the critic borrows the training GPUs four times per step and gives them back. The copies are not free: ppo_epochs: 4 means four rounds of moving the policy out and back per step, so raising it buys extra optimizer steps at the price of extra host-device traffic. If you are porting a PPO config, the two settings above (one chunk per step, and critic batch size equal to prompts × generations) are the ones setup will reject you for.
