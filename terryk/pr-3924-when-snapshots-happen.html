When a rollout snapshot actually happens — and how much work you get back

Companion note for NVIDIA-NeMo/RL #3924. All permalinks pin the head 962a670a.

surviving a kill when snapshots happen

Two pages on this PR, one question each — this one: how often a snapshot is taken, and what a restart gets back.

Setting rollout_checkpointing.interval_s starts a timer that tries to save rollout state. "Tries" is the word that matters: most attempts can do nothing, and which ones succeed is decided by your trainer checkpoint settings, not by the interval you picked.

A snapshot has to be anchored to a durable trainer checkpoint, and the rule is an exact match — the directory for the step that just finished:

# _save_rollout_checkpoint anchor = self._checkpointer.checkpoint_dir / f"step_{self._train_steps}" if not anchor.is_dir(): return False # skipped; no fallback to an older step

single_controller.py :3408–3409 · the one exception is before the first step finishes, when a separate bootstrap/ anchor is used instead

_train_steps is bumped at the end of a step, and the trainer checkpoint is written right after. So the anchor exists during the step that follows a checkpoint, and stops existing the moment the next step ends. That single fact drives everything below.

Three cadences, same feature

SettingWhat happens
interval_s shorter than a step
1 min timer, 5 min steps		 Roughly 5 attempts per step. Each one that finds new work writes a snapshot, but keep_latest_k (default 2) immediately deletes all but the newest two. An attempt that finds nothing new returns without writing — there is a check that skips a save when the data plane has not changed.
interval_s longer than a step
13 min timer, 5 min steps		 One attempt every 13 minutes, anchored to whichever step last finished. The timer never causes a model checkpoint — those keep their own schedule from save_period. You just save rollout state less often than you make it.
save_period greater than 1
any timer		 The anchor only exists for one step out of every save_period. With save_period=5, four steps in five cannot be snapshotted at all, no matter how short the timer is. This is the case worth understanding, and it is the figure below.

What a restart gives you back

Restore reads the trainer checkpoint first, then looks for a snapshot inside that same directory whose manifest matches the step and trainer version it just loaded:

resolved_snapshot = resolve_latest_snapshot( Path(trainer_checkpoint_path), # step_5/ expected_train_step=save_state.current_step, # 5 expected_trainer_version=restored_trainer_version, # 5 expected_bootstrap_fingerprint=None, )

That is why the answer is "rollouts from partway through step 6" and not "rollouts from step 7". A snapshot only ever lives in the checkpoint directory it was anchored to, and nothing was written after 12 minutes. Rollouts that step 6's optimizer had already claimed are rolled back into replayable state as part of the save, so they are not lost twice — they come back as work still to do.

One useful consequence of the bootstrap/ anchor: if the crash had happened at 8 minutes, before any trainer checkpoint existed, you would still restart from the initial weights with the rollout progress made in the first 2 minutes. That is the only thing covering the start of a run, and it is deleted the moment a real checkpoint lands.

How to pick the two numbers. The timer alone does not decide how much work you keep — save_period does. With save_period=5 you can lose almost five steps of rollouts however short you make interval_s, because four steps in five have no anchor to attach to. If you are turning this on to survive mid-step crashes, set save_period=1 so every step leaves an anchor, then choose interval_s for how much of a single step you are willing to redo. The exemplar config says as much in a comment, but nothing warns you at startup if the two settings disagree.
