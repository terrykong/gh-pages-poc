Three pages on the SingleController path, one question each — this one: which finished rollouts a step trains on.

Rollouts for one step do not finish together — a long generation can outlive the weights it started from. Every sampler is one answer to "what do I do with a group that finished at an awkward moment?" Five groups, one decision point, five rules.

All five modes at once

Trainer is at weight 3. num_prompts_per_step = 2 (so a step trains on at most 2 groups), max_staleness_versions = max_lookahead_versions = 1, and the trainer will run a step on as few as one group (min_groups_for_streaming_train is below num_prompts_per_step). The weight window the window-based modes use is [3 − 1, 3] = [2, 3].

What the picture is really showing
Only one thing is different between the panels: which rule the sampler consults. The buffer is identical in all five, and so are the bars — they sit exactly where they sit in the timeline at the top. in_order asks "was this meant for step 3?", weight_fifo asks "is this the oldest weight still in the window?", ready_first asks "is this finished and not from the future?", and windowed asks "is this inside the window?" — with sample_freshest_first changing which end of the window it takes from.

Why in_order and weight_fifo agree here
They agree most of the time. start_weight and target_step are both stamped when the batch is dispatched, so "the batch meant for step 3" and "the oldest weight still in the window" usually name the same groups. They come apart when a weight bump lands part-way through reserving a batch, or when a batch is dispatched with no lookahead left to use. Note the small asymmetry in weight_fifo: it picks the target weight from every slot in the window, ready or not, so unready B pins the target at weight 2 and D and E wait even though they are sitting there finished (L528-540).
Every mode here runs a short step, and that is on purpose
min_groups_for_streaming_train is the floor. Set it below num_prompts_per_step and _finalize_selection hands back whatever is ready, so in_order trains on C alone instead of waiting for B. Set it equal to num_prompts_per_step and the same code returns None until the whole batch has landed — that is the sync recipe, and it is the setting that makes in_order waste nothing at all.
Pick ready_first when throwing away rollouts is the thing you cannot afford. It is the only mode whose evict never drops anything: a group that finishes late is still trained on, however far behind it is. The price is that its staleness has no bound — A was generated three weight versions ago and is used anyway. That is why setup makes ready_first require loss_fn.use_importance_sampling_correction: true and force_on_policy_ratio: false at any staleness setting, unlike the other modes, which only need the correction once you raise their staleness knob above 0 (config.py L1185-1195). Also note in_order is still the only mode PPO accepts (config.py L1117).
